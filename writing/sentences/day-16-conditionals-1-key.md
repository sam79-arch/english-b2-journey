## **PART 1: Fill-in (5 questions)**

### **Q1:** If you _______ (not/sleep) tonight, you _______ (be) exhausted tomorrow.

**Answer:** **don't sleep / will be**

**Explanation:**
- If-clause: "If you **don't sleep**" (present simple, negative)
- Result: "you **will be** exhausted" (will + be)
- **Logic:** Real future condition — if this thing happens, this will happen

**Form:** If + present simple → will + base verb

---

### **Q2:** If the bus _______ (arrive) on time, I _______ (reach) home before 6 PM.

**Answer:** **arrives / will reach**

**Explanation:**
- If-clause: "If the bus **arrives**" (present simple)
- Result: "I **will reach**" (will + reach)
- **Real situation:** A likely, realistic scenario

**Common error:** ❌ "If the bus will arrive..." (WRONG — never use "will" in if-clause)

---

### **Q3:** Unless you _______ (pay) the bill, they _______ (disconnect) your Internet.

**Answer:** **pay / will disconnect**

**Explanation:**
- **Unless = If not**
- "Unless you pay" = "If you don't pay"
- **Logic:** Real consequence — no payment → they will cut service

**Note:** "Unless" already means "if not", so we don't need "don't" with it

---

### **Q4:** If she _______ (apply) now, she _______ (get) the job offer next week.

**Answer:** **applies / will get**

**Explanation:**
- Likely scenario with "now" signal
- Present simple (applies) + will (will get)
- **Cause-effect:** Early application → job offer

---

### **Q5:** As long as you _______ (keep) practicing, your English _______ (improve).

**Answer:** **keep / will improve**

**Explanation:**
- **As long as = On the condition that = If**
- Present simple + will
- **Continuous condition:** As long as you continue → improvement will happen

## **PART 2: Transform to Conditional 1 (5 questions)**

### **Q6:** You study hard → You pass the exam.  

**Answer:** **"If you study hard, you will pass the exam."**

**Explanation:**
- Cause: study hard (present)
- Effect: will pass (future)
- Natural cause-effect relationship

---

### **Q7:** She calls me → I help her.  

**Answer:** **"If she calls me, I will help her."**

**Explanation:**
- **Simple offer/promise**
- If-clause: "She calls" (present simple)
- Result: "I will help" (commitment)

---

### **Q8:** They don't arrive soon → We leave without them.  

**Answer:** **"If they don't arrive soon, we will leave without them."** 

OR  

**"Unless they arrive soon, we will leave without them."**

**Explanation:**
- Negative condition
- Either form is correct (if not / unless)

---

### **Q9:** You save money now → You can afford a trip.  

**Answer:** **"If you save money now, you can afford a trip."**

OR  

**"If you save money now, you will be able to afford a trip."**

**Explanation:**
- **Note:** "Can" is also possible in result clause (not just "will")
- **Modal alternatives:** will/can/might/could all work
- Cause: save money → Effect: afford (possible)

---

### **Q10:** It doesn't rain tomorrow → We will go hiking.  

**Answer:** **"If it doesn't rain tomorrow, we will go hiking."**

**Explanation:**
- Already has "will" in the original → stays in result
- Negative if-clause: doesn't rain
- Result: will go hiking

## **PART 3: Identify (5 questions)**

### **Q11:** "If I were you, I would apologize." 

**Answer:** ❌ **Not C1** — This is **Conditional 2** (hypothetical present)

**Why?**
- "Were" signals Conditional 2
- "Would" signals Conditional 2
- Hypothetical situation, not real future

---

### **Q12:** "If you heat ice, it will melt." 

**Answer:** ✅ **C1** (or scientific fact)

**Why?**
- Present simple (heat) + will (melt)
- Real, always-true situation (scientific law)
- Even if phrased like general truth, structure is C1

---

### **Q13:** "If he had studied, he would have passed." 

**Answer:** ❌ **Not C1** — This is **Conditional 3** (past hypothetical)

**Why?**
- "Had studied" = past perfect (if-clause)
- "Would have passed" = would + have + V3 (result)
- About what didn't happen in past, too late to change

---

### **Q14:** "If you arrive early, we will start the meeting." 

**Answer:** ✅ **C1** (practical future condition)

**Why?**
- Present simple (arrive) + will (start)
- Real, likely future scenario
- Shows cause-effect for upcoming event

---

### **Q15:** "If you smoke, cancer will develop." 

**Answer:** ✅ **C1** (warning/consequence)

**Why?**
- Present simple + will
- Shows causal consequence (real medical fact)
- Even though serious, structure is Conditional 1

## **PART 4: Multiple Choice (5 questions)**

### **Q16:** _____ you call me, I _____ happy to help.

**Answer:** A) **If / will be**

**Explanation:**
- "If" is the conditional marker
- Will + be is the result clause
- Offer: "If you call, I will be happy"

**Why others are wrong:**
- B) "If / am" — Wrong! You can't use "am" in result clause; need "will be"
- C/D) "Unless" changes meaning — "Unless you call, I'll be happy" means you are happy only if they don't call (opposite)

---

### **Q17:** If the weather _____ good, _____ you come to the beach?

**Answer:** A) **is / will**

**Explanation:**
- If-clause: "the weather **is** good" (present simple)
- Result question: "**will** you come?" (will + you + base)

**Why others are wrong:**
- B) "will be / do" — Wrong forms (will be in if-clause, "do" instead of will)
- C) "is / do" — Missing "will" in question
- D) "will be / will" — Double will (one in if-clause, which is forbidden)

---

### **Q18:** Unless you _____ your homework, your teacher _____ angry.

**Answer:** A) **finish / will be**

**Explanation:**
- Unless = if not
- "Unless you finish" = negative condition
- Result: "will be angry"

**Incorrect options:**
- B) "will finish / will be" — Never use "will" after unless (or if)
- C) "finish / is" — No "will" in result
- D) "don't finish / will be" — Don't add "don't" after "unless" (unless already means "if not")

**Remember:** Unless ≠ "if not" (we don't say "if not" with the same verb)

---

### **Q19:** If it _____ tomorrow, the match _____ cancelled.

**Answer:** A) **rains / will be**

**Explanation:**
- If-clause: "If it **rains**" (present simple)
- Result: "will **be** cancelled" (passive voice: will + be + V3)

---

### **Q20:** They _____ the party if everyone _____ on time.

**Answer:** B) **will start / arrives**

**Explanation:**
- Condition first: "if everyone **arrives**" (present simple)
- Result: "They **will start**" (will + start)
- Note: This sentence is written with result first, then condition — both orders are fine

**Why not A?** "will arrive" in if-clause breaks the rule

**Answer:** **follow / will go**

**Explanation:**

- **Note:** "go" is used (not "goes") because of "nothing" (singular)

**If you want to improve your English, you need to practice daily.**

**Explanation:**
- Both answers acceptable:
  1. **want / will need** (strict C1 form)
  2. **want / need** (general truth — no modal)
- Second form is more natural for general advice

# 📚 Key Rules to Memorize

Commit these to memory:

1. **"If" = Present Simple, Never "Will"**  
   If + present simple → will + base verb
   ❌ Never: "If will you come..."

2. **Result Always Has "Will"**  
   Exception: Can use other modals (can, may, might)
   But "will" is most natural and safe

3. **"Unless" = "If Not"**  
   "Unless you pay" = "If you don't pay"
   But write it as "unless you pay" (not "unless you don't pay")

4. **Signal Words**  
   if, unless, as long as, provided that = C1 structures

5. **Real vs Hypothetical**  
   - C1 = Real, likely, possible future ✓
   - C2 = Hypothetical, imaginary present ✗
   - C3 = Hypothetical past ✗
